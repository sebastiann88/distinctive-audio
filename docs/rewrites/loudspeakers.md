<p>
 Loudspeakers are the most personal decision in a two-channel system.
 Amplifiers and sources matter, but the speakers are what actually
 move the air in your room, and the right pair depends as much on
 that room — and on the music you love — as on anything printed on a
 spec sheet. No two rooms sound alike, and no two listeners want
 quite the same thing from them.
 </p>
<p>
 If you are shopping for loudspeakers in Ottawa, we would make the
 case for doing it the old-fashioned way: sitting down in a proper
 listening room and hearing them with your own music. Distinctive
 Audio has been matching speakers to listeners from our showroom at
 903 Carling Avenue since 1986, and four decades have taught us one
 durable lesson — the speaker that wins on paper is not always the
 one that disappears in your living room.
 </p>
<h2>
 Loudspeakers in Ottawa: What We Carry and Why
 </h2>
<p>
 We keep our loudspeaker roster deliberately short. Rather than
 stocking a wall of brands, we carry a handful of lines we know
 intimately — well enough to voice a complete system around them,
 demonstrate them properly, and stand behind them years after the
 sale.
 </p>
<h3>
 MartinLogan: Our Electrostatic Specialty
 </h3>
<p>
 MartinLogan is the line we are best known for. Their electrostatic
 hybrids, such as the ESL 9, pair a near-massless electrostatic panel
 with a conventional powered woofer, so you get the transparency of a
 panel with a real bass foundation underneath it. Their Motion series
 brings a related philosophy — a fast, precise folded-motion tweeter
 — to conventional cabinets at friendlier prices, from stand-mount
 monitors to full towers.
 </p>
<h3>
 Unity Audio: Sealed-Box Honesty
 </h3>
<p>
 Unity Audio takes the opposite approach: sealed cabinets voiced for
 accuracy first. The Warrior V2 and Whitewater V2 deliver tight,
 articulate bass and a midrange that tells you exactly what is on the
 recording. If your listening leans toward voices, acoustic music,
 and small ensembles, they are worth a long audition.
 </p>
<h3>
 Audio Physic: In Stock and Ready to Hear
 </h3>
<p>
 We currently have Audio Physic Classic 15 floorstanders on the
 showroom floor. These German towers are compact enough for a
 real-world living room yet throw the wide, stable soundstage the
 brand is known for — a good example of how a slim speaker, properly
 placed, can sound much bigger than it looks.
 </p>
<h2>
 Bookshelf, Floorstanding, or Electrostatic?
 </h2>
<p>
 Before comparing brands, decide which type of speaker your room and
 your listening habits actually call for. Each format has a distinct
 set of strengths, and the room usually casts the deciding vote.
 </p>
<h3>
 Bookshelf Speakers
 </h3>
<p>
 <a href="/products/loudspeakers/bookshelf-speakers">Bookshelf speakers</a>
 are the right answer more often than their size suggests. In a small
 or medium room, a good stand-mounted pair images beautifully,
 pressurizes the space without overloading it, and leaves you room to
 add a <a href="/products/loudspeakers/subwoofers">subwoofer</a>
 later if you want the bottom octave. They do their best work on
 proper <a href="/products/loudspeakers/speaker-stands">speaker stands</a>
 rather than on furniture — height and isolation matter more than
 most buyers expect.
 </p>
<h3>
 Floorstanding Speakers
 </h3>
<p>
 <a href="/products/loudspeakers/floorstanding-speakers">Floorstanding speakers</a>
 move more air, so they reach deeper and play louder without strain.
 In a larger room they deliver the scale and weight that a compact
 monitor simply cannot, and they do it without a separate subwoofer
 to place and blend. The trade-off is that they interact more
 strongly with the room, which makes placement — and an honest
 audition — all the more important.
 </p>
<h3>
 Electrostatic Panels
 </h3>
<p>
 <a href="/products/loudspeakers/electrostatic-panel-speakers">Electrostatic panel speakers</a>
 are the specialty of the house. A panel radiates sound from a large,
 nearly weightless diaphragm rather than a heavy cone, and the result
 is a level of transparency and low-level detail that keeps listeners
 in the chair long after the demo was supposed to end. They ask a
 little more of the room and the amplifier, which is exactly why we
 keep them set up and playing rather than sitting in boxes.
 </p>
<h2>
 Electrostatic vs Dynamic Drivers: What You Actually Hear
 </h2>
<p>
 Most speakers use dynamic drivers — cones and domes driven by a
 voice coil. An electrostatic replaces that with a thin film
 suspended between two charged panels, driven evenly across its whole
 surface.
 </p>
<h3>
 How an Electrostatic Panel Works
 </h3>
<p>
 Because the diaphragm weighs almost nothing, it starts and stops
 almost instantly. In listening terms that means texture: the breath
 before a vocal phrase, the decay of a piano note, the sense that
 instruments occupy real positions in space. MartinLogan's curved
 panels also widen the listening window, so the effect is not
 confined to a single seat the way older flat panels were.
 </p>
<h3>
 Where Dynamic Drivers Hold the Advantage
 </h3>
<p>
 Dynamic speakers move more air per square inch, so they generally
 deliver more bass weight and higher output from a smaller footprint,
 and they are less particular about placement. This is why
 MartinLogan's hybrids pair a panel with a dynamic woofer — and why,
 for many rooms, a well-executed dynamic speaker like the Unity Audio
 monitors or the Audio Physic towers is the better match. Neither
 technology wins in the abstract; your room and your music decide.
 </p>
<h2>
 Matching Speakers to Ottawa Homes
 </h2>
<p>
 We have set up systems across the Ottawa–Gatineau region for a long
 time, and the housing stock here spans everything from downtown
 condo towers to century-old brick homes in the Glebe to open-plan
 builds in the suburbs. Each presents a different acoustic problem.
 </p>
<h3>
 Condos and Apartments
 </h3>
<p>
 Shared walls and concrete floors reward speakers with controlled,
 articulate bass over sheer output. A sealed monitor or a modest
 floorstander, positioned well, gives you satisfying listening at
 reasonable levels — and keeps the relationship with your neighbours
 intact. Isolation under the speakers or stands helps here too,
 keeping bass energy out of the building's structure.
 </p>
<h3>
 Brick, Plaster, and Drywall
 </h3>
<p>
 Older Ottawa homes with brick and plaster walls are acoustically
 stiff: bass stays in the room, and boundary reinforcement is strong,
 so speakers often want to sit farther from the walls. Newer drywall
 construction absorbs and leaks more low-frequency energy, which can
 leave the same speaker sounding leaner. This is one reason we ask
 about your room before we recommend anything — the building itself
 is part of the system.
 </p>
<h3>
 Placement and Room Acoustics
 </h3>
<p>
 Placement is free performance. Distance from the front wall sets the
 bass balance, distance between the speakers and toe-in set the
 soundstage, and the first reflection points at the side walls decide
 how clean the midrange stays. Panels need breathing room behind them
 because they radiate rearward as well. When you buy from us, this is
 not left as an exercise for the reader: we talk through your room,
 your furniture, and your seating before the speakers ever go home.
 </p>
<h2>
 How to Audition Speakers Properly
 </h2>
<p>
 An audition is not a formality — it is the whole point. Here is how
 to get real information out of one.
 </p>
<h3>
 What to Bring
 </h3>
<p>
 Bring your own music, not audiophile test tracks. Choose four or
 five recordings you know so well you could hum the room tone: one
 with a voice you love, one with real bass, one that is densely
 mixed, one that is quiet and spare. Rough dimensions of your room —
 even a phone photo — help us set up something representative.
 </p>
<h3>
 How Long to Listen
 </h3>
<p>
 Longer than feels polite. First impressions favour speakers that are
 bright and forward; the pair you can live with for a decade reveals
 itself over a full unhurried session, and sometimes a second visit.
 We book demos so that nobody is standing over you waiting for a
 decision — that is what an appointment at our Carling Avenue
 showroom is for.
 </p>
<h3>
 Listen Past the First Impression
 </h3>
<p>
 Switch back and forth between candidates at matched volume, listen
 from off-axis seats as well as the sweet spot, and pay attention to
 whether you are following the music or analyzing the sound. The
 right speaker makes you reach for the next album. If a pair impresses
 you for ten minutes and tires you in forty, that is your answer.
 </p>
<h2>
 The Case for Two Channels Done Right
 </h2>
<p>
 We are a two-channel shop by conviction. A carefully chosen stereo
 pair, matched to the room and fed by good
 <a href="/products/amplification">amplification</a>, produces a
 deeper illusion of real music than a larger number of lesser
 channels ever will. Speakers and amplifiers succeed as a pair: an
 electrostatic panel wants an amplifier that is comfortable with its
 load, and a sealed monitor comes alive with clean, unforced power.
 Whether that means a tube integrated from Unison Research or
 PrimaLuna or a solid-state design from Audiolab, we voice the match
 in the showroom rather than guessing from spec sheets.
 </p>
<h2>
 Trade In and Move Up
 </h2>
<p>
 Loudspeakers are rarely a final purchase — tastes deepen, rooms
 change, and the itch to upgrade is part of the hobby. Our trade-in
 program gives your current speakers a fair value against the next
 pair, and our used and consignment stock means the pair you traded
 finds its way to a listener who will appreciate it. It is one more
 reason a relationship with a local shop beats a courier box: the
 people who sold you the first pair are still here for the third.
 </p>
<p>
 The best way to choose loudspeakers is still to hear them.
 <a href="/contact">Book a showroom demo</a> at 903 Carling Avenue,
 bring the music that matters to you, and take the time to listen —
 we have been doing it this way in Ottawa since 1986.
 </p>

### question field-uid: 89415568-1e17-45b6-9a94-45633d1a9541
Are MartinLogan electrostatic speakers worth the premium?
### answer field-uid: 6882284a-6822-4a88-89bf-6f1a1b0f45fd
For listeners who value transparency and low-level detail, yes — an electrostatic panel resolves texture that most conventional drivers smooth over. The honest way to decide is to hear one: we keep MartinLogan set up and playing in our Carling Avenue showroom so you can judge with your own music.

## BLOCK 5908 (faqItem)
### question field-uid: 89415568-1e17-45b6-9a94-45633d1a9541
What is the difference between bookshelf and floorstanding speakers?
### answer field-uid: 6882284a-6822-4a88-89bf-6f1a1b0f45fd
Floorstanding speakers move more air, so they play deeper and louder without a subwoofer; bookshelf speakers trade that extension for easier placement and better manners in smaller rooms. In a typical Ottawa condo, a good stand-mounted pair often sounds better than towers the room cannot support.

## BLOCK 5909 (faqItem)
### question field-uid: 89415568-1e17-45b6-9a94-45633d1a9541
How far should speakers be from the wall?
### answer field-uid: 6882284a-6822-4a88-89bf-6f1a1b0f45fd
Start with at least half a metre from the front wall and adjust by ear — closer reinforces bass, farther away deepens the soundstage. Electrostatic panels radiate from the back as well as the front, so they want closer to a metre of breathing room behind them.

## BLOCK 5910 (faqItem)
### question field-uid: 89415568-1e17-45b6-9a94-45633d1a9541
Do I need a separate power amplifier for my speakers?
### answer field-uid: 6882284a-6822-4a88-89bf-6f1a1b0f45fd
Not usually — a well-chosen integrated amplifier drives most speakers beautifully, and that is where we start most systems. What matters is the match between the speaker's sensitivity and impedance, your room size, and how loud you listen, which is exactly what a showroom audition settles.
