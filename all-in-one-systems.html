Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about integrating an all-in-one system.

What exactly is an all-in-one system? expand_more

An all-in-one system combines multiple hi-fi separates into a single chassis. Typically, this includes a preamplifier, a power amplifier, and source components like a network streamer, DAC (Digital-to-Analog Converter), and sometimes a CD player. You simply connect speakers to have a complete audio setup.

Does it stream and play CDs? expand_more

Most modern all-in-one systems feature robust network streaming capabilities (Wi-Fi/Ethernet) to access services like Tidal, Qobuz, and Spotify. Some models, like the Audiolab Omnia, also integrate a dedicated CD transport, allowing you to enjoy both digital files and physical media from the same device.

What kind of speakers do I need? expand_more

You will need passive speakers. The all-in-one system provides the necessary amplification. We recommend matching the amplifier's power output (wattage) with the speakers' sensitivity and impedance requirements. Our team can help you find the perfect pairing during a listening demo.

Can I add a turntable later? expand_more

Yes, many all-in-one systems include standard analog RCA inputs. Some even feature a built-in MM (Moving Magnet) phono stage specifically for turntables. If the system lacks a phono stage, you can simply add an external phono preamplifier between the turntable and the all-in-one system.
