Frequently Asked Questions

Expert guidance on choosing the right solid-state integrated amplifier for your system.

How does solid-state sound differ from a tube amplifier? expand_more

Solid-state integrateds are generally prized for tight bass grip, high damping factor and very low noise, giving a clean, controlled and dynamic presentation. Tube amplifiers tend to add a warmer, more rounded harmonic character. Neither is objectively "better" — it comes down to your speakers and listening taste, which is exactly what an in-store demo helps you decide.

What is the difference between Class A, Class AB and Class D? expand_more

Class A runs its output devices continuously for the lowest distortion, but it runs hot and is less efficient. Class AB blends Class A behaviour at low levels with greater efficiency at higher output, which is why it is the most common topology in quality integrateds. Class D switches very quickly for high efficiency, cool running and compact, often high-power designs. Modern examples of all three can sound excellent.

How much power do I need for my speakers? expand_more

Match the amplifier to your speakers' sensitivity and impedance. Efficient speakers (around 90 dB and up) can sing on modest wattage, while lower-sensitivity or 4-ohm designs benefit from an amplifier with plenty of current and stable power delivery. Your room size and how loud you listen also matter — we are happy to pair the right integrated to your speakers in person.

Do these amplifiers include a built-in DAC or phono stage? expand_more

It varies by model. Many modern integrateds add an onboard DAC, Bluetooth or streaming, and some include a moving-magnet phono stage for turntables — but others are kept purist with line-level inputs only. Check each model's feature list, and if you need digital or vinyl playback built in, let us know so we can recommend the right configuration.
